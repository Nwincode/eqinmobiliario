# Buying Property in Guatemala from the US

Whether you are a Guatemalan living in the United States or an American planning to retire in Antigua or on Lake
Atitlán, you can buy a home in Guatemala almost entirely from a distance. This guide walks through the steps in
the order they happen, and the points where buyers most often make mistakes.

## 1. Choose the property remotely

Start with the [homes for sale](/en/properties/for-sale/) and their photos, prices and locations. Before you make
an offer:

- Ask for a **live video tour**, not just a recorded clip, so you can see the street, the neighbors and anything
  you want to check.
- Ask for the property's **registry numbers** (*finca, folio and libro*). You need them for the title search in
  step 3.
- Compare prices in the same area. Our area pages show the price range and the median price when there are
  enough listings.

Foreigners can own property in Guatemala on the same terms as citizens, except in the border strips and the
State's reserved areas described in step 5 [verify].

## 2. Give someone a power of attorney

If you won't travel to sign, you need a **power of attorney** (*mandato*) in favor of a relative or your
attorney in Guatemala. There are two ways to get it:

- **Before a US notary, with an apostille.** Guatemala joined the Hague Apostille Convention in 2017 [verify]: you
  sign before a notary and have the document apostilled by the Secretary of State of your state. In Guatemala, a
  notary formalizes it (*protocolización*) and it is recorded in the Electronic Registry of Powers of Attorney
  [verify]. A Spanish translation may be required [verify].
- **At a Guatemalan consulate.** The consul can act as a notary. Book an appointment early.

Make the power specific: what your agent may do (buy, sign the deed, pay taxes, take possession) and for which
property. An unlimited power of attorney is a risk.

## 3. Run a title search at the Property Registry

Before you send any deposit, get a **certificate from the Registro General de la Propiedad** (RGP), the national
property registry. It shows:

- The **registered owner**, who must be the person selling to you.
- Any **mortgages, liens or court notes** on the property.
- The **registered size and boundaries**, which should match what you are shown.

Some western departments are recorded at the Second Registry in Quetzaltenango [verify]. Also ask the seller for
the latest **IUSI** receipt and paid-up utility and HOA bills.

## 4. Know the taxes and closing costs

- **Stamp tax (*timbres fiscales*), 3 %**: applies to the purchase of a resale property, on the value in the
  deed [verify].
- **VAT (IVA), 12 %**: applies to the **first sale of a new property** by the developer; in that case the 3 %
  stamp tax does not also apply to the same sale [verify].
- **IUSI**: the annual municipal property tax, based on the assessed value in brackets of 2, 6 and 9 per
  thousand [verify]. It is paid quarterly.
- **Notary fees and registration fees**: ask for a written quote before you sign.

Who pays what is agreed in the purchase agreement (*promesa de compraventa*). Usually the buyer pays the taxes on
the purchase and the seller delivers the property free of debts.

## 5. Beachfront and lakefront: the State's reserved areas

Guatemala's Constitution reserves strips of land along the coasts, lakes and navigable rivers for the State
(*reservas territoriales del Estado*). For example, **200 meters around the shores of lakes** such as Atitlán and
Amatitlán; along the ocean the strip is wider [verify: Article 122 refers to three kilometers from the high-tide
line]. In these areas you don't buy the land: you get a **lease from OCRET**, the office that manages the State's
reserved areas. If you are offered a beach or lakefront house, ask whether it is in a reserved area and ask to see
the current lease.

## 6. Sign the deed before a Guatemalan notary

The purchase is made in a **public deed** (*escritura pública*) before a notary in Guatemala. The notary:

1. Checks your agent's power of attorney and the registry certificate.
2. Drafts the deed with the price, payment terms and handover.
3. Calculates and pays the transfer tax (stamp tax or VAT).
4. Files the deed with the Property Registry to record the property in your name.

You own the property once it is **recorded in the Registry**, not just when the deed is signed. Keep a copy of
the deed (*testimonio*) and the recording stamp.

## 7. Paying from the US safely

- Pay by **bank wire**, never in cash. Guatemalan banks offer US dollar accounts, so you can wire dollars to the
  seller's or developer's account.
- The bank may ask for **proof of source of funds** and an anti-money-laundering form (IVE) above certain
  amounts [verify]. Keep your bank statements at hand.
- Don't send money before you have the registry certificate and a signed purchase agreement that says what
  happens to your deposit if the sale doesn't close.

## 8. Financing options

- **Guatemalan banks**: some lend to Guatemalans living abroad with verifiable US income [verify]; options for
  foreign nationals are more limited. Down payments usually run 10 % to 30 %.
- **FHA Guatemala** (Instituto de Fomento de Hipotecas Aseguradas, not the US FHA): long-term insured mortgages
  through participating banks [verify eligibility for non-residents].
- **Developer financing** on new projects: pay the down payment in installments during construction.
- **From the US**: a US mortgage won't cover a home in Guatemala; some buyers use a home equity line of credit
  (HELOC) or a cash-out refinance on their US home. Talk to your financial advisor.

You can estimate a monthly payment on each property for sale.

## Checklist

1. Pick the property on a live video tour and get its registry numbers.
2. Sign a power of attorney (US notary + apostille, or consulate).
3. Get the Property Registry certificate.
4. Budget for taxes: 3 % stamp tax or 12 % VAT, IUSI and notary.
5. Ask whether it sits in a State reserved area (coast or lake).
6. Sign the deed before a Guatemalan notary and record it.
7. Pay by wire, with documented source of funds.
